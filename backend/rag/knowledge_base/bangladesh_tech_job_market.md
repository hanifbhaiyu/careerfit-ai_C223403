# The Bangladesh Technology Job Market

## Where the jobs are

Hiring concentrates in a few clusters. Dhaka holds the large majority of technology roles, particularly in Gulshan, Banani, Bashundhara and Mohakhali, where most software companies and the engineering arms of banks and telecoms sit. Chattogram has a smaller but growing base, weighted towards logistics, shipping technology, and the back offices of manufacturing groups. Sylhet and Khulna have limited local demand, so candidates there compete mainly for remote positions.

The employer types differ in what they reward:

- **Local software services companies** build for overseas clients. They hire in volume, train juniors, and value breadth. Pay is moderate, learning is fast, and project variety is high.
- **Product companies and startups**, including the ride-hailing, e-commerce, fintech, and edtech firms, pay above the services average for the same seniority and expect stronger fundamentals. Interview processes are longer and more technical.
- **Banks, telecoms and corporates** pay well at senior levels, have slower processes, and favour candidates with formal qualifications and enterprise stack experience.
- **Multinational delivery centres and overseas remote employers** pay in a different band entirely, but the bar is high and competition includes candidates from across South Asia.
- **Freelance and contract work** remains a large share of technology income nationally, particularly in web development, design, and digital marketing.

## What employers screen for

Across employer types, a few consistent patterns appear in hiring decisions:

**Demonstrable projects outweigh coursework.** A deployed application with real users, or a GitHub repository with commit history over months, moves a fresher's application further than a strong CGPA alone. Reviewers open repositories, and an empty profile linked on a CV counts against the candidate.

**English writing ability is a filter, not a bonus.** Most technology employers serve overseas clients or work in English-language documentation. Written communication is assessed from the CV and cover email before any interview, and poor written English removes candidates who are technically capable.

**A relevant degree helps but does not gate entry.** Self-taught developers and bootcamp graduates are hired regularly, particularly in web and mobile development. Banks and government-adjacent employers are the exception and generally require a CSE or related degree.

**Internship experience converts.** A substantial share of junior hires at established companies come through internships. For students, an internship at a recognised firm is the highest-leverage single move available.

## Skills in persistent demand

Demand shifts, so verify current figures against live listings rather than trusting any static list. That said, several areas have been consistently sought for multiple years:

- Web development with JavaScript, React, and Node.js, which remains the largest single category of openings.
- Backend work in Python, PHP with Laravel, or Java Spring, depending on the employer's legacy stack.
- Mobile development, with Flutter widely adopted for cross-platform work.
- Data roles, including analytics, SQL proficiency, and increasingly machine learning engineering.
- Cloud and DevOps skills, where supply is notably thinner than demand and pay premiums are correspondingly larger.
- Quality assurance and automated testing, an underserved area that offers an accessible entry route.

## Salary structure

Salary in this market is negotiated case by case and varies by a wide multiple between employer types for identical titles. Published averages are unreliable because they mix services, product, and multinational employers into one figure.

The dependable structural facts:

- Compensation rises steeply between the first and third year for candidates who change employers, and much more slowly for those who stay.
- Product companies and remote overseas employers pay materially above local services firms at every level.
- Cloud, DevOps, data engineering, and security specialisations command a premium over general web development at the same seniority.
- Many offers include festival bonuses, which candidates should confirm are included in any stated annual figure rather than assumed.

Always check current listings and recent salary reports before naming a number in a negotiation. Anchoring on a figure that is a year out of date costs money.

## Applying effectively

Job boards carry a large volume of listings, but referral remains the highest-conversion channel. A significant portion of roles at desirable employers are filled before or without a public posting.

Practical approach:

1. Apply to public listings within the first two days of posting. Response rates fall sharply after the first week as the shortlist fills.
2. Build a LinkedIn presence and connect with engineers at target companies rather than only with recruiters. An engineer's internal referral carries more weight than a cold application.
3. Attend meetups and developer community events, which are active in Dhaka and growing in Chattogram. These convert to referrals more often than online applications do.
4. For remote overseas roles, expect to compete on portfolio and communication rather than credentials, and expect a longer process with multiple technical stages.

## Freelancing as an entry route

For candidates who cannot secure a first full-time role, freelance work on international platforms builds the two things employers screen for: a portfolio of delivered work and evidence of client communication in English. Bangladesh has a large freelance workforce, and freelance delivery history is increasingly accepted as legitimate experience by local employers, provided the candidate can describe the technical substance of the work rather than only the platform rating.
